"When is the best time to go to Mexico?" is the question we are asked most, and our honest answer is always: it depends where, and what for. Mexico stretches across more than 3,000 kilometres, from desert to rainforest, sea level to high plateau. On the same day in July it can be 35 degrees and humid in Mérida, pleasantly fresh in San Cristóbal, and raining every afternoon in Mexico City.

There is no bad month to visit. There are just months that suit some trips better than others.

## The big picture

- **Dry season** runs roughly from **November to April** across most of the country. Clear skies, comfortable temperatures, and the highest prices and busiest crowds.
- **Rainy season** runs roughly from **June to October**. In the highlands and the south, that usually means a sharp afternoon storm and a sunny morning. Landscapes turn green, and prices drop.
- **Hurricane season** officially runs from **1 June to 30 November** on the Caribbean and Gulf coasts, and from **15 May to 30 November** on the Pacific. The highest risk is from mid-August to mid-October. Direct hits on any given place are rare, but storms can disrupt plans.
- **Sargassum**, the seaweed that washes up on the Caribbean coast, tends to peak between about April and September. It varies hugely from year to year and beach to beach.

| Season | Best for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| December to April | Beaches, whales, monarchs, colonial cities | Peak prices, Christmas and Easter crowds |
| May | Cenotes, ruins without crowds | Intense heat in the Yucatán and interior |
| June to August | Whale sharks, green landscapes, Guelaguetza | Afternoon rains, sargassum, heat on the coast |
| September to October | Low prices, quiet ruins, Independence Day | Peak hurricane risk, heaviest rain |
| November | Día de Muertos, the start of dry season | Booking well ahead for festivals |

## January

One of the most settled months of the year. The Yucatán is warm but not punishing, the Pacific coast is sunny and dry, and the colonial highlands have bright days and cold nights, close to freezing in places like San Cristóbal and San Miguel. **Monarch butterfly** season is in full swing in the mountains of Michoacán and the State of Mexico, and **whale season** is underway in Baja and around Puerto Vallarta. After the New Year rush, the second half of January can be surprisingly good value.

## February

Our favourite month for wildlife. Gray whales are at their peak in the calving lagoons of Baja California Sur, where curious mothers sometimes bring their calves right up to the small boats. Humpbacks breach off Los Cabos and in the Bay of Banderas. The monarchs are at their densest, often coating whole fir trees. **Carnaval** brings parades to Mérida, Veracruz and Mazatlán in the days before Lent.

## March

Still dry and warm, with temperatures climbing. The **jacarandas** turn Mexico City purple from late February into March, and it is beautiful. Around the spring equinox, crowds gather at **Chichén Itzá** to watch the shadow of a serpent appear to crawl down the steps of El Castillo. Watch out for US spring break, which fills Cancún, Los Cabos and Puerto Vallarta in March. The monarchs begin heading north towards the end of the month.

## April

Beautiful weather, but plan carefully around **Semana Santa**, Holy Week, which falls in March or April depending on the year. It is one of Mexico's biggest holidays: beaches, bus stations and colonial towns fill with Mexican families, prices rise and hotels sell out months in advance. The processions in towns like San Miguel de Allende and Taxco are moving to witness, if you book early. Heat builds in the Yucatán through the month, and sargassum can start to appear.

## May

The hottest month in much of the country before the rains, with Mérida and the interior of the Yucatán regularly hitting 40 degrees. It is a great month for cenotes, which stay cool all year, and ruins are quiet if you start early. Late May is the official start of the Pacific hurricane season, and whale shark season around Holbox and Isla Mujeres gets going towards the end of the month.

## June

The rains arrive in the highlands and the south, usually as afternoon downpours. Mexico City, Oaxaca and Chiapas turn green, and mornings are fresh and clear. **Whale shark** season is in full swing off Holbox and Isla Mujeres, one of the most extraordinary wildlife encounters anywhere. The Atlantic hurricane season begins on 1 June, though early-season storms are usually less of a concern. Expect heat and humidity on the coasts.

## July

Mexican school holidays begin, so beach towns get busier with domestic travellers. In Oaxaca, the **Guelaguetza** fills the last two Mondays of the month with regional dance, music and food, and it is one of the best festivals in the country. Afternoon storms continue in the highlands, but they rarely spoil a day if you plan your sightseeing for the morning. Whale sharks remain a highlight.

## August

The rainy season is at its height in the south and the centre, and the landscapes are lush. Whale shark season continues, often at its peak. In Baja's **Valle de Guadalupe**, the **Fiestas de la Vendimia** celebrate the grape harvest. Hurricane risk starts to rise in the second half of the month, so build a little flexibility into any beach plans.

## September

The quietest month for international tourism, and the one with the best prices. It is also peak hurricane season on both coasts, with some of the heaviest rain of the year. On the night of 15 September, plazas across Mexico fill for **El Grito**, the cry of independence, followed by parades on the 16th. It is a joyful time to be in a city like Mexico City, San Miguel de Allende or Guanajuato. Whale sharks wind down by mid-month.

## October

Rains ease through the month, and by late October much of the country is fresh, green and sunny. Hurricane risk remains in the first half. Guanajuato hosts the **Festival Internacional Cervantino**, one of Latin America's largest arts festivals, filling the alleys with music and theatre. Towards the end of the month, the build-up to Día de Muertos begins, with marigold markets and altars appearing everywhere.

## November

One of our favourite months of all. The dry season begins, the landscapes are still green, and on **1 and 2 November** Mexico celebrates **Día de Muertos**, the Day of the Dead. Families build altars, or ofrendas, with marigolds, candles and the favourite foods of loved ones, and spend nights in decorated cemeteries. Oaxaca, Pátzcuaro and Mixquic are among the most atmospheric places to experience it, and Mexico City holds a huge parade. Book months ahead and come with respect: this is a family occasion first. The **monarch butterflies** arrive in their mountain forests early in the month.

## December

Peak season. The weather is lovely almost everywhere, but Christmas and New Year bring the highest prices and the biggest crowds of the year, especially on the coasts. On 12 December, pilgrims arrive at the Basilica of Guadalupe in Mexico City for the feast of the Virgin of Guadalupe. From 16 to 24 December, neighbourhoods hold **posadas**, with candlelit processions, piñatas and ponche. Whale season begins again in Baja and on the Pacific coast as the gray and humpback whales arrive.

## Our quick recommendations

- **For beaches on the Caribbean:** December to April, with November as a quieter shoulder month.
- **For colonial cities and the highlands:** October to April for dry days, though the green season has its own charm.
- **For whales:** January to March. **For whale sharks:** June to August. **For monarchs:** December to February.
- **For festivals:** late July for the Guelaguetza, mid-September for Independence, and 1 to 2 November for Día de Muertos.
- **For value:** late April to June, and September to mid-November, with a little flexibility.
